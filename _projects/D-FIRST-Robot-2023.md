In 2023, I oversaw the design of my high school's FIRST robotics team 3655 Gone Fishin'.

I worked closely with the mentors to design a robot that is feasible to build in our team's capabilities in CAD and faciliated with the manufacturing and assembly of the robot. 


<img src="{{ '/assets/images/Robot at Competition.JPG' | relative_url }}"
     style="display: block; width: 80%; margin: 0rem auto;">

The robot is composed of a ground intake, where the swerve drive can drive over the game piece (the "notes") and pick them up from the ground. The intake feeds the game piece into the shooter, and the motors attached on the shooter launches the game piece when the robot is ready to shoot. I also included space to mount the climbers onto the robot for the endgame, where the robot needs to cling onto a piece horizontal chain and hang in the air to score points. 

Features:
- The low center of gravity on this robot made it much easier for our driver to develope more aggressive playing style. 
- Most metal plates on the robot were CNC machined,

Why did the team end up using COTS (Commercial Off The Shelf) swerve drives? 
- General lack of machining experience on the team
- Gears would need to be bought anyways
- Lack of experience slows down building cycle, want to have reliable system working early

Why was I working on the switch to swerve drive? 
- Much faster in speed
- Much more mobile --- can translate in any direction, and can rotate while translating
- Opens up for more strategies like defense during matches
